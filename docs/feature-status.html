Headline. Phases 0–4 of the roadmap are complete and in the product, backend and frontend. 1,321 backend tests and a 43-case Playwright suite gate every merge across six CI jobs. 36 migrations.

What is not done divides into two kinds, and the difference matters when planning: work that needs code (multi-currency, localization, the AI takeoff feature itself) and work that needs an account or a decision (Stripe keys, accounting OAuth approval, mail DNS, an accuracy bar for takeoff). The second kind cannot be closed by building anything.

How to read a status here

"Shipped" means merged, tested and reachable — not "written". Several gates in this codebase exist because something looked done and was not: an alert naming a metric that does not exist, a policy that would satisfy a "has a policy" check and leak everything, a test suite that passes trivially over an empty set. Each of those is now asserted against something the system generates rather than against a transcribed list.

"Awaiting credentials" is not a code estimate. Stripe, the two accounting providers and email are each complete behind a real client that is selected only when its own credentials are configured, with a fake as the default. Nothing further gets built by waiting; someone has to open an account or publish a DNS record.

The one genuinely open engineering question is the accuracy bar for AI takeoff, and it is now measurable rather than arguable. What it needs is a handful of real plan sets and the takeoffs your estimators produced from them.

Where a bug was found, a gate was added. Making one line nullable (migration 0035) silently broke three separate queries that joined through it, each carrying a comment saying it could never be null; all three were found afterwards, separately. Two sweeps now assert those properties over the code itself rather than relying on a fourth careful reading — one for join correctness, one for line ordering. The second found a call site nobody had noticed on the day it was written.
